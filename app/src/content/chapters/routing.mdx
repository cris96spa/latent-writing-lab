## From a teaching claim to a model

A sparse router can select a subset of experts and assign that subset weights that
sum to one. This example uses a small illustrative model, not experimental measurements.
The Hugging Face MoE article provides broader context [@hf-moe].

Given scores $z_i$ and a positive temperature $T$, compute

$$
p_i = \frac{\exp(z_i/T)}{\sum_j \exp(z_j/T)}.
$$

Select the $k$ largest scores, giving a set $S$, and renormalize:

$$
w_i = \begin{cases}p_i / \sum_{j \in S}p_j & i \in S,\\0 & i \notin S.\end{cases}
$$

For fixed scores, changing a positive temperature preserves their ordering. It changes
how concentrated the weights are, not which experts rank highest. Change the active
expert count and temperature to inspect that distinction.

The default publication state shows the normalized mixture with two active experts
and temperature one. Each inactive expert receives zero weight, and the active
weights sum to one. Selection marks and numerical labels supplement color.
